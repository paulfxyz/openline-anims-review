      The thinking

      A hero animation has three jobs

      Today's header does one of them. Every proposal was designed against these three, then scored on six criteria.

      01
      Prove the claim

      The headline promises AI switching across Tier-1 networks. The visual should let someone verify that in two seconds, without reading a word.

      02
      Never sit still

      A 15-second loop with one comet means most visitors see a still picture. Anything past ~1.5 s of stillness is a dead header.

      03
      Stay cheap

      It sits in the LCP zone of a page that ships in 20 languages. Inline SVG only: no video, no new library, and it honours reduced-motion.

      Scores are my judgement, not measurements — they exist to make the trade-offs arguable. Hover a column header for its definition.

      My pick
      6 · Failover — with 1 · Live Handoff as the safe version

      Failover is the only option that makes the competitor's weakness visible: a network dies, Openline reroutes, the user never notices. Live Handoff tells the same story with no alarm colour and the smallest diff against what is live.

      If the goal is conversion

      Put a narrative header on feature pages and 9 · eSIM Wallet on the storefront. A wallet of networks is an object people believe they are buying; a diagram is not.

      If the goal is credibility

      7 · Auction Board or 10 · Kinetic HUD, wired to real pricing later. Live numbers read as infrastructure; illustration reads as marketing. Both are also the lightest to ship.
